Three types of problems will appear on Midterm 2: short answer definitions and meaning, computational and applied problems.

A. Definitions and meaning

Write a short precise answer (one or two sentences, or a formula). No long essays.

A1. What does it mean to say that \(A\) is an \(m\times n\) matrix? What size is each column of \(A\)?

A2. Give the column view formula for \(Ax\) when \(A\) has columns \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) and \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\).

A3. In one sentence: what does it mean to interpret \(A\) as a transformation from \(\mathbb{R}^n\) to \(\mathbb{R}^m\)?

A4. Name three structured matrix types from class (beyond “square”) and one practical consequence of structure for at least one of them.

A5. Translate “\(Ax=b\) has a solution” into a statement about the column space of \(A\).

A6. What is reduced row echelon form (RREF)? What are pivot and free variables?

A7. When does \(Ax=b\) have (i) no solution, (ii) a unique solution, (iii) infinitely many solutions? Answer in terms of RREF / consistency / free variables for a square or rectangular system.

A8. Write the model for a linear dynamical system used in class. What do the state \(x_t\) and matrix \(A\) represent?

A9. When is the product \(AB\) defined? What are the dimensions of \(AB\) if \(A\) is \(m\times n\) and \(B\) is \(n\times p\)?

A10. Why is matrix multiplication not generally commutative? Give a one-line reason (sizes or a counterexample idea).

A11. How does \(A^k x\) relate to the dynamical system \(x_{t+1}=Ax_t\)?

A12. What does it mean for \(A\) to be invertible? How does invertibility relate to uniqueness of solutions of \(Ax=b\)?

A13. Why is solving \(Ax=b\) generally preferable to forming \(A^{-1}\) and computing \(A^{-1}b\)?

A14. Define the column space \(C(A)\) and the null space \(N(A)\).

A15. Define \(\operatorname{rank}(A)\). How does rank relate to \(\dim C(A)\) and \(\dim N(A)\) for an \(m\times n\) matrix?

A16. For a \(2\times 2\) matrix \(\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\ c&d\end{bmatrix}\), write \(\det A\). What does \(|\det A|\) mean geometrically for the image of the unit square?

A17. True or false, with a one-line reason: “If \(\det A=0\), then \(A\) is invertible.”

A18. True or false, with a one-line reason: “If \(AB=I\), then \(BA\) must also equal \(I\) for square \(A,B\) of the same size.” (You may assume the course’s usual finite-dimensional setting.)

A19. Light review (Quiz 5): What is a basis of a subspace \(V\)? What is \(\dim V\)?

A20. For each expression, say whether the result is a scalar, an \(n\)-vector, an \(m\times n\) matrix, a set of vectors, or undefined (brief reason if undefined). Assume sizes make sense unless noted.

  1. \(Ax\) when \(A\) is \(m\times n\) and \(x\) is an \(n\)-vector

  2. \(A+B\) when \(A\) and \(B\) are both \(m\times n\)

  3. \(AB\) when \(A\) is \(2\times 3\) and \(B\) is \(2\times 3\)

  4. \(A^{\mathsf{T}}\) when \(A\) is \(m\times n\)

  5. \(\det A\) when \(A\) is \(2\times 2\)

  6. \(A^{-1}\) when \(A\) is singular \(2\times 2\)

  7. \(C(A)\)

  8. \(N(A)\)

  9. \(\operatorname{rank}(A)\)

  10. \(A^2\) when \(A\) is \(3\times 2\)

  11. \(ABx\) when \(A\) is \(2\times 2\), \(B\) is \(2\times 2\), and \(x\) is a \(2\)-vector

  12. residual \(Ax-b\) when \(Ax=b\) is consistent

B. Computational

Show your work for each calculation.

Matrices, \(Ax\), and transforms

B1. Let \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1&2\\ 0&3&1\end{bmatrix},\quad x=\begin{bmatrix}2\\ 1\\ -1\end{bmatrix}. \] Compute \(Ax\) using the column-combination view.

B2. Same \(A\) as B1. What are the input and output dimensions of the map \(x\mapsto Ax\)?

B3. Let \[ R=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\ 1&0\end{bmatrix}. \] Compute \(R\begin{bmatrix}1\\ 0\end{bmatrix}\) and \(R\begin{bmatrix}0\\ 1\end{bmatrix}\). Describe the geometric effect of \(R\) on \(\mathbb{R}^2\).

B4. Let \[ S=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\ 0&1\end{bmatrix}. \] Compute \(S\begin{bmatrix}1\\ 0\end{bmatrix}\) and \(S\begin{bmatrix}0\\ 1\end{bmatrix}\). What kind of transform is this (scaling / reflection / shear / rotation)?

B5. Let \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}2&0\\ 0&3\end{bmatrix},\quad B=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\ 1&0\end{bmatrix}. \] Compute \(A+B\), \(2A\), and \(A^{\mathsf{T}}\).

B6. Is \[ T=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\ 2&1\end{bmatrix} \] symmetric? Triangular? Diagonal?

Systems, elimination, RREF

B7. Write the linear system \[ \begin{aligned} x+2y&=5,\\ 3x-y&=1 \end{aligned} \] as \(Ax=b\). Give \(A\), \(x\), and \(b\).

B8. Solve the system in B7 by hand (elimination or substitution). How many solutions?

B9. The augmented matrix \([A\mid b]\) row-reduces to \[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 & | & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & | & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & | & 0 \end{bmatrix}. \] Identify pivot variables and free variables. Write the general solution parametrically.

B10. Same RREF idea, but the last row is \([0\ 0\ 0\ |\ 4]\). How many solutions? Why?

B11. Reduce \[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & | & 3 \\ 2 & 4 & | & 6 \end{bmatrix} \] to RREF. Describe the solution set of the corresponding system.

B12. For \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\ 2&4\end{bmatrix},\quad b=\begin{bmatrix}3\\ 7\end{bmatrix}, \] is \(b\) in \(C(A)\)? Brief reason (no need for full RREF if you see the issue quickly).

Dynamics

B13. Let \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}0.5&0.5\\ 0.5&0.5\end{bmatrix},\quad x_0=\begin{bmatrix}1\\ 0\end{bmatrix}. \] Compute \(x_1=Ax_0\) and \(x_2=Ax_1\).

B14. A two-state system has transition matrix \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}0.9&0.2\\ 0.1&0.8\end{bmatrix} \] (urban/rural fractions as in lecture). If \(x_0=(0.6,0.4)\), compute \(x_1=Ax_0\) and interpret the entries.

B15. For the system \(x_{t+1}=Ax_t\) with the \(A\) from B13, express \(x_3\) in terms of \(A\) and \(x_0\) using a matrix power (do not expand the power by hand unless you want to check).

Matrix products, powers, cost

B16. Let \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\ 0&1\end{bmatrix},\quad B=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\ 1&0\end{bmatrix}. \] Compute \(AB\) and \(BA\). Are they equal?

B17. With \(A\) from B16, compute \(A^2\).

B18. Dimensions: \(A\) is \(4\times 3\), \(B\) is \(3\times 2\), \(C\) is \(2\times 5\). Is \((AB)C\) defined? What size is it? Is \(A(BC)\) defined?

B19. Suppose \(a\) is a \(1000\)-vector, \(b\) is a \(1000\)-vector, and \(c\) is a \(1000\)-vector. Compare forming \((ab^{\mathsf{T}})c\) versus \(a(b^{\mathsf{T}}c)\) in words: which forms a huge intermediate matrix, and which is cheaper?

B20. Let \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\ 0&1\end{bmatrix}. \] Compute \(A^2\) and \(A^3\). Guess a formula for \(A^k\).

Inverses

B21. Find the inverse of \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\ 0&1\end{bmatrix} \] (if it exists) by finding a matrix \(B\) with \(AB=I\).

B22. Is \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\ 2&4\end{bmatrix} \] invertible? Give two different reasons (e.g. determinant, dependent columns, or null space).

B23. Solve \[ \begin{bmatrix}1&1\\ 0&1\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}x_1\\ x_2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}3\\ 2\end{bmatrix} \] without forming an inverse explicitly (back substitution / elimination is fine).

Rank, column space, null space

B24. Let \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\ 2&0\\ 0&1\end{bmatrix}. \] Describe \(C(A)\). Is \(b=(1,2,3)\) in \(C(A)\)? Is \(b=(1,3,0)\) in \(C(A)\)?

B25. Same \(A\) as B24. Find \(N(A)\) (solve \(Ax=0\)). Give a basis for \(N(A)\), or state that only \(0\) is in the null space.

B26. The RREF of a \(3\times 4\) matrix \(A\) is \[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}. \] What is \(\operatorname{rank}(A)\)? Which original columns form a basis for \(C(A)\) (pivot columns)? Find a basis for \(N(A)\).

B27. For the matrix in B26, what is \(\dim N(A)\)? Check that \(\operatorname{rank}(A)+\dim N(A)=n\).

Determinants

B28. Compute \[ \det\begin{bmatrix}3&1\\ 2&4\end{bmatrix}. \]

B29. Compute \[ \det\begin{bmatrix}2&1&0\\ 0&-3&4\\ 0&0&5\end{bmatrix}. \]

B30. The linear map given by \(A=\begin{bmatrix}2&0\\ 0&3\end{bmatrix}\) sends the unit square to a rectangle. What is the area of that rectangle? How does this relate to \(\det A\)?

B31. Does \[ \det\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\ 2&4\end{bmatrix}=0 \] tell you that the columns are dependent? That \(A\) is noninvertible? That \(Ax=b\) fails for some \(b\)?

C. Applied / harder

Show reasoning. These mix ideas or need a careful choice of tool.

C1. Let \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\ 0&1\end{bmatrix},\quad b=\begin{bmatrix}4\\ 2\end{bmatrix}. \]

  1. Solve \(Ax=b\).
  2. Interpret \(A\) as a transformation of \(\mathbb{R}^2\): what does it do to the standard basis vectors?
  3. Why does this system have a unique solution?

C2. A café stores each day as a \(3\)-vector (coffee sold, pastry sold, total items), where total \(=\) coffee \(+\) pastry.

  1. Write a matrix \(A\) (or describe its columns) so that “feature vector” \(=Ax\) for an underlying \(2\)-vector \(x=(\text{coffee},\text{pastry})\).
  2. Are the three feature directions dependent? How does that show up in \(\operatorname{rank}(A)\)?
  3. Why might this hurt a method that treats the three features as independent coordinates?

C3. Dynamics and products. Let \(x_{t+1}=Ax_t\) with \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\ 0&0\end{bmatrix},\quad x_0=\begin{bmatrix}1\\ 1\end{bmatrix}. \]

  1. Compute \(x_1\), \(x_2\), and \(x_3\).
  2. Compute \(A^2\). How does that explain \(x_2\)?
  3. In one sentence: what long-run behavior do you see?

C4. Composition. A point is first reflected across the \(y\)-axis by \[ F=\begin{bmatrix}-1&0\\ 0&1\end{bmatrix}, \] then rotated \(90^\circ\) counterclockwise by \[ R=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\ 1&0\end{bmatrix}. \]

  1. Which product, \(RF\) or \(FR\), applies reflection first, then rotation? (Remember: \(x\mapsto Fx\) then \(x\mapsto Rx\) means \(R(Fx)=(RF)x\).)
  2. Compute that product and apply it to \((1,0)\).

C5. Cost. You need \(y=ab^{\mathsf{T}}c\) where \(a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}^n\) and \(n\) is large.

  1. What is the size of the intermediate matrix \(ab^{\mathsf{T}}\)?
  2. What scalar do you get from \(b^{\mathsf{T}}c\) first, and how do you finish?
  3. Which order would you choose, and why?

C6. Rank and consistency. Let \[ A=\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 & 6 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}. \]

  1. Argue that the first two columns are dependent.
  2. Find \(\operatorname{rank}(A)\) (you may reduce or reason about independent columns).
  3. For which of \(b=(0,0,0)\), \(b=(3,6,1)\), \(b=(1,0,0)\) is \(Ax=b\) guaranteed / possible / impossible? Brief reasons.

C7. Null space and dependence. Columns of \(A\) are dependent if and only if \(N(A)\) contains a nonzero vector. Use that fact on \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\ 2&4\end{bmatrix}: \] find a nonzero \(x\) with \(Ax=0\), and write the corresponding dependence relation among the columns.

C8. Determinants and invertibility. For each matrix, compute the determinant and say whether it is invertible:

  1. \(\begin{bmatrix}4&1\\ 0&2\end{bmatrix}\)
  2. \(\begin{bmatrix}1&-1\\ -2&2\end{bmatrix}\)
  3. \(\begin{bmatrix}5&0&0\\ 0&-1&0\\ 0&0&3\end{bmatrix}\)

C9. Mixed “which tool?” — for each task, name the best primary tool from: \(Ax\) / column combination, geometric transform, RREF / free variables, dynamical iteration \(A^k x\), matrix product / composition, computational reordering, inverse, rank / column space / null space, determinant.

Task Tool
“Will this \(2\times 2\) squash area to zero?”
“Is \(b\) a combination of the columns?”
“Write all solutions when there is a free variable”
“Undo a reversible transform”
“Where is the population after 10 steps?”
“Reflect, then rotate”
“Avoid building a huge outer product”
“How many independent columns?”

C10. Software interpretation. A classmate runs an RREF command and gets \[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 & | & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & | & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & | & 0 \end{bmatrix} \] for \([A\mid b]\). They conclude “the only solution is \(x=0\).” What did they miss? Give the correct general solution.

C11. Geometry of \(\det\). Let \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\ 0&1\end{bmatrix}. \]

  1. Sketch the images of \(e_1=(1,0)\) and \(e_2=(0,1)\) under \(A\), and the parallelogram they span.
  2. Compute \(\det A\). Does the parallelogram have area \(1\)?
  3. Is \(A\) invertible? Relate your answer to the parallelogram not collapsing to a line.

C12. Basis refresh + column space. Let \(V=C(A)\) for \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2&3\\ 0&0&0\\ 1&2&3\end{bmatrix}. \]

  1. Find a basis for \(V\) and \(\dim V\).
  2. Is \((1,0,1)\) in \(V\)? Is \((1,1,1)\) in \(V\)?
  3. Find a basis for \(N(A)\).

C13. Build a transition matrix. Two classrooms: each day, \(80\%\) of students in Room 1 stay, \(20\%\) move to Room 2; \(70\%\) in Room 2 stay, \(30\%\) move to Room 1. Let \(x_t=(r_1,r_2)\) be the counts (or fractions) in the two rooms.

  1. Write the matrix \(A\) in \(x_{t+1}=Ax_t\).
  2. If \(x_0=(100,0)\), compute \(x_1\).
  3. Does \(A\) preserve the total number of students? Check \(x_1\) entries sum.

Answers

Check only after a closed-book attempt. Update your definitions/formulas list for every miss.

A — Definitions (key points)

A1. \(A\) has \(m\) rows and \(n\) columns; each column is an \(m\)-vector.

A2. \(Ax=x_1 a_1+\cdots+x_n a_n\).

A3. \(A\) maps each input \(x\in\mathbb{R}^n\) to the output \(Ax\in\mathbb{R}^m\).

A4. e.g. identity, diagonal, triangular, symmetric, sparse, zero — sparse saves storage/ops; triangular makes determinants/back-substitution easy; etc.

A5. \(b\in C(A)\) (equivalently: \(b\) is a linear combination of the columns).

A6. RREF is a row-echelon form with pivots \(1\) and zeros above/below pivots. Pivot variables correspond to pivot columns; free variables are the non-pivot unknowns.

A7. (i) inconsistent row \([0\ \cdots\ 0\mid c]\) with \(c\neq 0\); (ii) consistent and no free variables; (iii) consistent with at least one free variable.

A8. \(x_{t+1}=Ax_t\); \(x_t\) is the state, \(A\) is the transition / update matrix.

A9. Inner dimensions match (\(A\)’s columns \(=\) \(B\)’s rows); \(AB\) is \(m\times p\).

A10. Order matters: \(AB\) and \(BA\) need not both be defined, and even when both are, they need not be equal.

A11. \(x_k=A^k x_0\) (after \(k\) steps).

A12. There exists \(A^{-1}\) with \(AA^{-1}=A^{-1}A=I\). For square \(A\), invertible iff \(Ax=b\) has a unique solution for every \(b\).

A13. Forming \(A^{-1}\) is more work / less stable; solving the system directly is the standard approach.

A14. \(C(A)=\operatorname{span}\{\text{columns of }A\}\); \(N(A)=\{x:Ax=0\}\).

A15. Rank \(=\) dimension of the column space (\(=\) number of pivots). For \(m\times n\) matrix: \(\dim C(A)=\operatorname{rank}(A)\) and \(\dim N(A)=n-\operatorname{rank}(A)\).

A16. \(ad-bc\); absolute value \(=\) area scaling factor for the unit square (signed area accounts for orientation).

A17. False — \(\det A=0\) means noninvertible.

A18. True (in this course’s finite-dimensional square-matrix setting): a one-sided inverse is a two-sided inverse.

A19. Independent spanning list; dimension \(=\) number of vectors in any basis.

A20. (a) \(m\)-vector (b) \(m\times n\) matrix (c) undefined — inner dimensions \(3\neq 2\) (d) \(n\times m\) matrix (e) scalar (f) undefined — singular, no inverse (g) set of vectors (h) set of vectors (i) scalar (nonnegative integer) (j) undefined — \(A\) is not square / \(A A\) sizes fail (k) \(2\)-vector (l) \(m\)-vector that is \(0\) when consistent (here the zero vector)

B — Easy computations

B1. \(Ax=2\begin{bmatrix}1\\ 0\end{bmatrix}+1\begin{bmatrix}-1\\ 3\end{bmatrix}+(-1)\begin{bmatrix}2\\ 1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-1\\ 2\end{bmatrix}\).

B2. Input \(\mathbb{R}^3\), output \(\mathbb{R}^2\).

B3. \(R e_1=(0,1)\), \(R e_2=(-1,0)\); \(90^\circ\) counterclockwise rotation.

B4. \(S e_1=(1,0)\), \(S e_2=(2,1)\); shear (horizontal shear).

B5. \(A+B=\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\ 1&3\end{bmatrix}\); \(2A=\begin{bmatrix}4&0\\ 0&6\end{bmatrix}\); \(A^{\mathsf{T}}=A\) (already symmetric diagonal).

B6. Symmetric: yes. Triangular: no. Diagonal: no.

B7. \(A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\ 3&-1\end{bmatrix}\), \(x=\begin{bmatrix}x\\ y\end{bmatrix}\), \(b=\begin{bmatrix}5\\ 1\end{bmatrix}\).

B8. Unique solution: e.g. \(x=1\), \(y=2\) (check: \(1+4=5\), \(3-2=1\)).

B9. Pivots \(x_1,x_2\); free \(x_3=t\). General: \(x=(3-2t,\ 1+t,\ t)\).

B10. No solutions — inconsistent (\(0=4\)).

B11. RREF \(\begin{bmatrix}1&2&|&3\\ 0&0&|&0\end{bmatrix}\); infinitely many: \(x_1=3-2t\), \(x_2=t\).

B12. No — second row of \(A\) is \(2\times\) first, but \(7\neq 2\cdot 3\).

B13. \(x_1=(0.5,0.5)\); \(x_2=(0.5,0.5)\).

B14. \(x_1=(0.9\cdot 0.6+0.2\cdot 0.4,\ 0.1\cdot 0.6+0.8\cdot 0.4)=(0.62,0.38)\); urban / rural fractions tomorrow.

B15. \(x_3=A^3 x_0\).

B16. \(AB=\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\ 1&0\end{bmatrix}\), \(BA=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\ 1&2\end{bmatrix}\); not equal.

B17. \(A^2=\begin{bmatrix}1&4\\ 0&1\end{bmatrix}\).

B18. Yes, size \(4\times 5\); yes, \(A(BC)\) also \(4\times 5\).

B19. \((ab^{\mathsf{T}})c\) builds a \(1000\times 1000\) matrix; \(a(b^{\mathsf{T}}c)\) only needs a dot product then a scale — much cheaper.

B20. \(A^2=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\ 0&1\end{bmatrix}\), \(A^3=\begin{bmatrix}1&3\\ 0&1\end{bmatrix}\); \(A^k=\begin{bmatrix}1&k\\ 0&1\end{bmatrix}\).

B21. \(B=\begin{bmatrix}1&-2\\ 0&1\end{bmatrix}\) (check \(AB=I\)).

B22. Not invertible: \(\det=0\); columns dependent (\(c_2=2c_1\)); nontrivial null space, e.g. \((2,-1)\).

B23. From second row \(x_2=2\); first row \(x_1+x_2=3\) so \(x_1=1\).

B24. \(C(A)=\operatorname{span}\{(1,2,0),(0,0,1)\}=\{(x,2x,z)\}\). \((1,2,3)\): yes; \((1,3,0)\): no (middle entry must be twice the first).

B25. \(Ax=0\) forces \(x_1=0\), \(x_2=0\); \(N(A)=\{0\}\) (two pivot columns; only the trivial null vector).

B26. Rank \(2\); pivot columns 1 and 3 of the original \(A\). Free variables \(x_2=s\), \(x_4=t\). From RREF: \(x_1+2s+3t=0\), \(x_3-t=0\), so \[ x=s\begin{bmatrix}-2\\ 1\\ 0\\ 0\end{bmatrix}+t\begin{bmatrix}-3\\ 0\\ 1\\ 1\end{bmatrix}. \] Basis for \(N(A)\): those two vectors.

B27. \(\dim N(A)=2\); \(2+2=4=n\).

B28. \(12-2=10\).

B29. Product of diagonal: \(2\cdot(-3)\cdot 5=-30\).

B30. Area \(6\); equals \(|\det A|=6\).

B31. Yes; yes; yes (column space is not all of \(\mathbb{R}^2\)).

C — Harder

C1. (a) \(x_2=2\), \(x_1=2\). (b) \(e_1\mapsto e_1\), \(e_2\mapsto(1,1)\) — shear. (c) Square invertible matrix / unique RREF pivots in both columns / \(\det A=1\neq 0\).

C2. (a) e.g. \(A=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\ 0&1\\ 1&1\end{bmatrix}\). (b) Yes — third row \(=\) sum of first two; \(\operatorname{rank}(A)=2\). (c) Redundant feature double-counts / distorts distance-based methods.

C3. (a) \(x_1=(1,0)\), \(x_2=(0,0)\), \(x_3=(0,0)\). (b) \(A^2=0\), so \(x_2=A^2 x_0=0\). (c) Dies to zero in two steps.

C4. (a) \(RF\). (b) \(RF=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\ -1&0\end{bmatrix}\); \((RF)(1,0)=(0,-1)\).

C5. (a) \(n\times n\). (b) Scalar \(b^{\mathsf{T}}c\), then multiply by \(a\). (c) \(a(b^{\mathsf{T}}c)\) — avoids the huge matrix.

C6. (a) Column 2 \(=2\times\) column 1. (b) \(\operatorname{rank}(A)=2\) (e.g. columns 1 and 3 are independent; column 2 is redundant). (c) \(b=(0,0,0)\) always solvable; \(b=(3,6,1)\) is column 3, so solvable; \(b=(1,0,0)\) not in \(C(A)\) (any combination has second entry equal to twice the first).

C7. e.g. \(x=(2,-1)\); \(2c_1-c_2=0\).

C8. (i) \(8\), invertible; (ii) \(0\), not invertible; (iii) \(-15\), invertible.

C9. Determinant; column space / \(Ax=b\); RREF / free variables; inverse; dynamical iteration \(A^k x\); matrix product / composition; computational reordering; rank / independent columns.

C10. Free variable \(x_3=t\); solution \(x=(-2t,\ t,\ t)\), not only zero.

C11. (a) Images \((1,0)\) and \((1,1)\); parallelogram is the unit-area shear of the square. (b) \(\det A=1\); area \(1\). (c) Yes invertible — parallelogram has positive area / does not collapse.

C12. (a) Basis e.g. \(\{(1,0,1)\}\); \(\dim=1\). (b) \((1,0,1)\) yes; \((1,1,1)\) no (middle entry). (c) \(x_1+2x_2+3x_3=0\); basis e.g. \(\{(-2,1,0),(-3,0,1)\}\).

C13. (a) \(A=\begin{bmatrix}0.8&0.3\\ 0.2&0.7\end{bmatrix}\) (columns: where each room’s students go — column 1 from Room 1, column 2 from Room 2). (b) \(x_1=(80,20)\). (c) Yes — total \(100\) preserved (columns sum to \(1\)).

## A. Definitions and meaning

Write a short precise answer (one or two sentences, or a formula). No long essays.

**A1.** What does it mean to say that $A$ is an $m\times n$ matrix? What size is each column of $A$?

**A2.** Give the **column view** formula for $Ax$ when $A$ has columns $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ and $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$.

### A — Definitions (key points)

**A1.** $A$ has $m$ rows and $n$ columns; each column is an $m$-vector.

**A5.** $b\in C(A)$ (equivalently: $b$ is a linear combination of the columns).

**A6.** RREF is a row-echelon form with pivots $1$ and zeros above/below pivots. Pivot variables correspond to pivot columns; free variables are the non-pivot unknowns.

**A7.** (i) inconsistent row $[0\ \cdots\ 0\mid c]$ with $c\neq 0$; (ii) consistent and no free variables; (iii) consistent with at least one free variable.
