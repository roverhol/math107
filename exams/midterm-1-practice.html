Three types of problems will appear on Midterm 1: short answer definitions and meaning, computational and applied problems.

A. Definitions and meaning

Write a short precise answer (one or two sentences, or a formula). No long essays.

A1. What is an \(n\)-vector? Give one non-math example from class (with units on the entries).

A2. What is a linear combination of vectors \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\)?

A3. Define the inner product (dot product) of two \(n\)-vectors. When are two vectors orthogonal?

A4. When is a function \(f\colon\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}\) called linear? How does affine differ?

A5. Define the Euclidean norm \(\lVert x\rVert\) and the distance between \(x\) and \(y\).

A6. Write the formula for cosine similarity of nonzero \(a\) and \(b\). What range of angles \(\theta=\angle(a,b)\) do we use?

A7. What is a (distance) nearest neighbor of a query \(x\) among candidates \(z_1,\ldots,z_m\)?

A8. In one sentence: what is the goal of clustering \(N\) vectors into \(k\) groups?

A9. State the definition of linear dependence of \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\). What does independent mean?

A10. Define \(\operatorname{span}\{a_1,\ldots,a_k\}\).

A11. What is a subspace of \(\mathbb{R}^n\)? Why is the line \(y=2x+1\) in \(\mathbb{R}^2\) not a subspace?

A12. What is a basis of a subspace \(V\)? What is \(\dim V\)?

A13. When is a list \(q_1,\ldots,q_k\) orthonormal? If it is an orthonormal basis of a subspace containing \(v\), how do you find the coefficients of \(v\)?

A14. True or false, with a one-line reason: “If \(\lVert a-b\rVert\) is small, then \(\angle(a,b)\) must be near \(0\).”

A15. True or false, with a one-line reason: “Every spanning list for \(V\) is a basis of \(V\).”

A16. \(k\)-means vocabulary.

  1. In \(k\)-means, what does the integer \(k\) stand for?
  2. After vectors have been assigned to \(k\) clusters, what is the formula for the center (centroid) of a nonempty cluster?
  3. Name the two steps that \(k\)-means alternates after an initial assignment (or after choosing initial centers).

A17. For each expression, say whether the result is a scalar, an \(n\)-vector, a set of vectors, or undefined (brief reason if undefined). Assume \(a,b,u,v\) are \(n\)-vectors and \(\alpha\) is a scalar, unless noted.

  1. \(a^{\mathsf{T}}b\) (dot product of two \(n\)-vectors)

  2. \(\lVert a\rVert\)

  3. \(a+b\)

  4. \(\alpha a\)

  5. cosine similarity of nonzero \(a\) and \(b\)

  6. \(\angle(a,b)\)

  7. \(\operatorname{span}\{a,b\}\)

  8. \(a^{\mathsf{T}}b+c\) when \(c\) is an \(n\)-vector

  9. \(a+b^{\mathsf{T}}\) (treating \(b^{\mathsf{T}}\) as a row)

  10. \(\mathrm{rms}(a)\)

  11. a \(k\)-means centroid of a nonempty cluster of \(n\)-vectors

  12. \(\dim\operatorname{span}\{a,b\}\)

  13. \(a^{\mathsf{T}}b\) when \(a\) is a \(2\)-vector and \(b\) is a \(3\)-vector

  14. unit vector \(\hat{a}=a/\lVert a\rVert\) when \(a\neq 0\)

B. Computational

Show your work for each calcualtion.

Vectors, combinations, dot products

B2. Write \(w=(5,1)\) as a linear combination of \(a=(1,0)\) and \(b=(1,1)\).

B3. Are \((1,2)\) and \((-2,-4)\) orthogonal? Compute the dot product.

B4. Let \(x=(2,1,-1)\). Compute \(\lVert x\rVert\) exactly (simplified radical OK).

B5. Distance between \(p=(1,1)\) and \(q=(4,-3)\).

B6. Cosine of the angle between \(a=(1,0)\) and \(b=(1,1)\). Angle in degrees?

B7. Unit vector in the direction of \((3,4)\).

B8. \(x=(1,-1,1,-1)\). Compute \(\mathrm{avg}(x)\) and \(\mathrm{rms}(x)\).

B9. Is \(f(x)=3x_1-x_2+2\) linear or affine? Brief reason.

B10. Is \(g(x)=x_1 x_2\) linear? Test with one superposition counterexample or explain.

B11. Score \(s=w^{\mathsf{T}}r\) with \(w=(0.5,0.5)\) and \(r=(80,90)\). What is \(s\)?

B12. Boolean vectors \(x=(1,0,1,1)\) and \(y=(1,1,0,1)\). What is \(\lVert x-y\rVert\)?

Nearest neighbors, scale, angle vs distance

B13. Query \(x=(1,2)\); candidates \(z_1=(0,2)\), \(z_2=(3,2)\), \(z_3=(1,3)\). Which is nearest in Euclidean distance? Give the three distances.

B14. Same \(x\) and candidates as B13, but replace every first coordinate \(t\) by \(10t\). Which is nearest now? Did it change?

B15. \(x=(1,0)\), \(z_1=(2,0)\), \(z_2=(0.5,0.5)\). Distance-NN and angle-NN of \(x\)? Same or not?

B16. Cosine similarity of \((2,1)\) and \((4,2)\). What does that tell you about the angle?

Dependence, span, basis

B21. Are \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\) independent in \(\mathbb{R}^2\)? Brief reason.

B22. Are \((2,4)\) and \((1,2)\) independent? If dependent, give a dependence relation.

B23. Find scalars not all zero with \(\beta_1(1,1)+\beta_2(1,-1)+\beta_3(0,2)=0\).

B24. Is \((3,3)\) in \(\operatorname{span}\{(1,1)\}\)? If yes, give the coefficient.

B25. Is \((1,0)\) in \(\operatorname{span}\{(1,1),(1,-1)\}\)? Coefficients or reason.

B30. Are \(\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigr)\) and \(\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}},-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigr)\) orthonormal?

C. Applied

Show reasoning. These mix ideas or need a careful choice of tool.

C1. Let \(p=(1,1)\), \(q=(10,10)\), \(r=(1,-1)\).

  1. Which of \(q,r\) is closer to \(p\) in Euclidean distance?
  2. Which is more similar to \(p\) in direction?
  3. In one sentence: when would you prefer angle over distance for “similarity”?

C2. A café stores each customer as (coffee count, pastry count). Someone adds a third feature “total items” \(=\) coffee \(+\) pastry.

  1. Are the three feature directions dependent? Explain.
  2. Why might this hurt nearest-neighbor recommendations that use Euclidean distance?

C3. Data: \(x_1=(0,0)\), \(x_2=(1,0)\), \(x_3=(0,1)\), \(x_4=(10,10)\). Run one \(k\)-means assignment with \(k=2\) and initial \(z_1=(0,0)\), \(z_2=(9,9)\). Which points go to which cluster? What goes wrong conceptually if you wanted two “local” clusters near the origin and ignored the outlier’s pull on a centroid later?

C4. Let \(a=(1,2)\), \(b=(2,1)\), \(c=(3,3)\).

  1. Show \(\{a,b,c\}\) is dependent by exhibiting a dependence relation.
  2. Show \(\{a,b\}\) is a basis of \(\mathbb{R}^2\).
  3. Express \(c\) in that basis (coefficients).

C5. Decide which are subspaces of \(\mathbb{R}^2\): (i) \(\operatorname{span}\{(2,1)\}\); (ii) all \((x,y)\) with \(y=3x+1\); (iii) \(\{(0,0)\}\); (iv) the unit circle. One sentence each.

C6. Feature vectors for documents (word counts) can have very different total lengths. Why is cosine similarity often preferred to Euclidean distance for “similar documents”?

C7. \(f(x)=\lVert x-c\rVert^2-\lVert x-d\rVert^2\) for fixed \(c,d\). Expand and show \(f\) is affine in \(x\) (find \(a\) and \(b\) in \(a^{\mathsf{T}}x+b\)).

C8. You normalize every candidate to unit length, then pick the Euclidean nearest neighbor to a query \(x\) that is already a unit vector. What comparison are you effectively making?

C9. Let \(V=\operatorname{span}\{(1,0,1),(0,1,1)\}\).

  1. Find \(\dim V\).
  2. Give one vector in \(\mathbb{R}^3\) not in \(V\), with a short reason.
  3. Is \((1,1,2)\) in \(V\)? If yes, give coefficients.

C10. Mixed “which tool?” — for each task, name the best primary tool from: linear combination, dot product, norm/distance, angle, nearest neighbor, \(k\)-means, span, independence, basis.

Task Tool
Weighted course score from a grade vector
“Is this third sensor redundant?”
“Which past day is most like today?”
“Group songs into two mood types”
“Can this vector be built from \(a\) and \(b\)?”
“Minimal independent spanning set for a plane through the origin”

C11. Graph — span. In \(\mathbb{R}^2\), sketch axes and draw \(a=(2,1)\) and \(b=(-1,2)\) as arrows from the origin.

  1. Sketch the line \(\operatorname{span}\{a\}\) (the whole line through \(a\), not just the arrow).
  2. Shade or describe \(\operatorname{span}\{a,b\}\). Is it all of \(\mathbb{R}^2\)? Why?
  3. Mark a point \(v\) that is in \(\operatorname{span}\{a\}\) and a point \(w\) that is not in \(\operatorname{span}\{a\}\).

C12. Graph — unit vectors and angle. Let \(u=(3,0)\) and \(v=(0,4)\).

  1. Sketch \(u\), \(v\), and the unit vectors \(\hat{u}=u/\lVert u\rVert\) and \(\hat{v}=v/\lVert v\rVert\) from the origin (same axes).
  2. What are \(\lVert\hat{u}\rVert\) and \(\lVert\hat{v}\rVert\)? What is \(\angle(\hat{u},\hat{v})\)?
  3. On your sketch, which looks “closer” to \(\hat{u}\): the tip of \(\hat{v}\), or the tip of \(v\)? Which comparison is Euclidean distance making? Which is angle making?

C13. Graph — closeness vs direction. Let \(p=(1,1)\), \(q=(3,3)\), and \(r=(1,0)\).

  1. Sketch \(p\), \(q\), and \(r\) from the origin.
  2. By eye (then check with a short computation): which of \(q,r\) is closer to \(p\) in distance? Which is closer in angle/direction?
  3. Draw the unit circle lightly and mark \(\hat{p}\), \(\hat{q}\), \(\hat{r}\). Explain in one sentence how the unit circle helps you see directional similarity.

A — Definitions (key points)

A1. List of \(n\) numbers (entries) with meaning/units; e.g. temperatures by hour, or (lat, lon).

A2. \(\beta_1 a_1+\cdots+\beta_k a_k\) for scalars \(\beta_i\).

A3. \(a^{\mathsf{T}}b=\sum_i a_i b_i\); orthogonal iff \(a^{\mathsf{T}}b=0\).

A4. Linear: \(f(\alpha x+\beta y)=\alpha f(x)+\beta f(y)\) (equivalently \(f(x)=a^{\mathsf{T}}x\)). Affine: \(a^{\mathsf{T}}x+b\) with possible nonzero \(b\).

A5. \(\lVert x\rVert=\sqrt{x^{\mathsf{T}}x}\); distance \(\lVert x-y\rVert\).

A6. \(a^{\mathsf{T}}b/(\lVert a\rVert\lVert b\rVert)\); \(\theta\in[0,\pi]\).

A7. Any \(z_j\) minimizing \(\lVert x-z_j\rVert\).

A8. Partition so same-group vectors are close (small within-group distances), with representatives.

A9. Dependent: nontrivial \(\beta\) with \(\sum\beta_i a_i=0\). Independent: only \(\beta=0\) works.

A10. All linear combinations of \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\).

A11. Nonempty set closed under linear combinations (contains \(0\)). Line \(y=2x+1\) misses \(0\) / not closed under scaling.

A12. Independent spanning list; dimension \(=\) number of vectors in any basis.

A13. Unit and pairwise orthogonal; \(\beta_i=q_i^{\mathsf{T}}v\).

A14. False — e.g. near-origin opposite directions.

A15. False — spanning lists can be dependent.

A16. (a) The number of clusters (groups). (b) The mean of the vectors in that cluster: if cluster \(j\) contains \(x_{i_1},\ldots,x_{i_m}\), then \(c_j=\frac{1}{m}(x_{i_1}+\cdots+x_{i_m})\). (c) Assignment (each point to the nearest current center) and update (replace each center by the mean of its cluster).

A17. (a) scalar (b) scalar (c) \(n\)-vector (d) \(n\)-vector (e) scalar (f) scalar (an angle, in radians or degrees) (g) set of vectors (a subspace / span) (h) undefined — scalar plus vector (i) undefined (sizes/shapes don’t match for vector \(+\) row; not a valid \(n\)-vector sum in this course) (j) scalar (k) \(n\)-vector (l) scalar (a nonnegative integer) (m) undefined — lengths must match for a dot product (n) \(n\)-vector

## B. Computational

Show your work for each calcualtion.

### Vectors, combinations, dot products

**B1.** Let $u=(3,-1,2)$ and $v=(0,4,-1)$. Compute $u+v$, $2u-v$, and $u^{\mathsf{T}}v$.

**B2.** Write $w=(5,1)$ as a linear combination of $a=(1,0)$ and $b=(1,1)$.

**B3.** Are $(1,2)$ and $(-2,-4)$ orthogonal? Compute the dot product.

**B4.** Let $x=(2,1,-1)$. Compute $\lVert x\rVert$ exactly (simplified radical OK).

**B5.** Distance between $p=(1,1)$ and $q=(4,-3)$.
