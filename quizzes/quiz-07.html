Textbooks: Boyd & Vandenberghe (VMLS) · Lachniet Octave PDF · also on Resources.

Schedule

Draw on paper for every item that says draw. Label the original and the image, and mark \(e_1\) and \(e_2\) when you use them. A point lands at the tip of \(A\) times that vector. The columns of \(A\) are where the standard basis lands: column \(j\) is \(Ae_j\).

Core

Fluency (complete prompts — practice these by hand)

C7.1. Let \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}3&1\\0&2\end{bmatrix}. \] Compute \(Ae_1\) and \(Ae_2\) for \(e_1=(1,0)\) and \(e_2=(0,1)\). In one sentence, how do those two images sit inside \(A\)?

C7.2. Reflection through the \(x_1\)-axis sends \(e_1=(1,0)\mapsto(1,0)\) and \(e_2=(0,1)\mapsto(0,-1)\). Write \(A\), compute \(A(2,3)\), and draw \(e_1\), \(e_2\), and both images on one set of axes.

C7.3. Rotation by \(90^\circ\) counterclockwise. Write the matrix. Where do \(e_1\) and \(e_2\) land? Draw those two arrows, then draw the unit square with vertices \((0,0)\), \((1,0)\), \((1,1)\), \((0,1)\) and its image.

C7.4. Stretch by \(A=\operatorname{diag}(2,1/2)\). Where do \(e_1\) and \(e_2\) land? Draw the same unit square and its image.

C7.5. Slant (shear) by \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{bmatrix}. \] Where do \(e_1\) and \(e_2\) land? Draw the unit square and its image. What shape is the image?

C7.6. Flip through the \(x_2\)-axis: build \(A\) by tracking \(e_1\) and \(e_2\). The triangle has vertices \((0,0)\), \((2,0)\), and \((1,1)\). Compute the three image points and draw the original triangle and the image.

C7.7. Let \(X=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&1\\0&1&1\end{bmatrix}\) (columns are three vertices) and \(A=\operatorname{diag}(2,1)\). Compute \(AX\). Draw the original triangle and the image, and describe the effect in one sentence.

C7.8. The rectangle has vertices \((0,0)\), \((2,0)\), \((2,1)\), \((0,1)\). Apply the \(90^\circ\) counterclockwise rotation from C7.3 to each vertex. Draw the original rectangle and the image.

C7.9. Using the shear in C7.5, where does the single point \((0,2)\) land? You may use that \((0,2)=2e_2\). Draw the point and its image.

Textbook Core

  • B 7.2, 7.3, 7.4

Stretch

  • B 7.1, 7.8
  • Sketch the unit circle \(x_1^2+x_2^2=1\) and its image under \(A=\operatorname{diag}(2,1/2)\). You do not need a perfect curve: mark where \(e_1\), \(-e_1\), \(e_2\), and \(-e_2\) go, and join them.

What a quiz item might change

Which map (rotation, flip, stretch, or slant), which shape (basis arrows, square, triangle, or rectangle), or asking only for the images of \(e_1\) and \(e_2\) before any other points.
