Schedule

Core

Fluency (complete prompts — practice these by hand)

C9.1. Let \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\0&3\end{bmatrix}. \] (a) Compute \(A^{-1}\) using the \(2\times 2\) formula (or by solving \(AB=I\)). (b) Check \(A^{-1}A=I\). (c) Solve \(Ax=(4,3)\) using \(x=A^{-1}b\).

C9.2. Decide whether each matrix is invertible. Give a one-line reason (dependent columns, \(Ax=0\), or \(\det\)).

  1. \(\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{bmatrix}\)

  2. \(\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\2&4\end{bmatrix}\)

  3. \(\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}\)

C9.3. True or false, with a one-line reason: for a single right-hand side \(b\), forming \(A^{-1}\) explicitly and then computing \(A^{-1}b\) is generally preferable to solving \(Ax=b\) with a solver / elimination.

C9.4. Let \[ A=\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 2 & 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}. \] The RREF of \(A\) is \[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \] (after row swaps / scaling as needed — use this RREF). Find \(\operatorname{rank}(A)\), a basis for \(C(A)\) using original columns, and a basis for \(N(A)\).

C9.5. Let \[ A=\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}. \] Describe \(C(A)\) in words. Is \(b=(1,2,3)\) in \(C(A)\)? Find a basis for \(N(A)\) (or state that \(N(A)=\{0\}\)).

C9.6. True or false, with a one-line reason: if \(\operatorname{rank}(A)=n\) for an \(m\times n\) matrix with \(m\ge n\), then \(Ax=0\) has only the zero solution.

C9.7. Compute each determinant.

  1. \(\det\begin{bmatrix}3&1\\0&2\end{bmatrix}\)

  2. \(\det\begin{bmatrix}1&4&0\\0&-2&5\\0&0&3\end{bmatrix}\)

  3. \(\det\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\4&2\end{bmatrix}\)

C9.8. Let \(A=\begin{bmatrix}0&-2\\1&0\end{bmatrix}\). Compute \(\det A\). Where do \(e_1\) and \(e_2\) go under \(x\mapsto Ax\)? What area does the unit square map to?

C9.9. True or false, with a one-line reason: if \(\det A=0\) for square \(A\), then \(A\) is not invertible and \(Ax=b\) fails to have a unique solution for every \(b\).

C9.10. Connect the ideas. For square \(A\), match each statement to the equivalent view (write the letter):

    1. \(A\) is invertible
    1. \(\operatorname{rank}(A)=n\) (full)
    1. \(\det A\neq 0\)
    1. \(N(A)=\{0\}\)

Which of (ii)–(iv) are equivalent to (i)?

C9.11 (cumulative, light). Let \(A=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{bmatrix}\). What geometric map is \(x\mapsto Ax\)? Is \(A\) invertible? What is \(\det A\)?

Textbook Core

  • B 11.1, 11.2, 11.4, 11.10
  • S 3.5 #1 and #2 (rank / subspace portions used in class)
  • L Ch. 1 Ex. 3(a), 3(b), and 3(d): determinant, rank, and inverse portions

Stretch

B 11.15, 11.25; area scaling from \(|\det A|\) for a new \(2\times 2\) map (C-M2 style).

What a quiz item might change

A new \(2\times 2\) matrix, an RREF with a free variable, whether \(\det A=0\) in context, or asking for \(C(A)\) vs \(N(A)\) from the same matrix.

## Core

### Fluency (complete prompts — practice these by hand)

**C9.1.** Let
$$
A=\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\0&3\end{bmatrix}.
$$
(a) Compute $A^{-1}$ using the $2\times 2$ formula (or by solving $AB=I$).
(b) Check $A^{-1}A=I$.
(c) Solve $Ax=(4,3)$ using $x=A^{-1}b$.

**C9.2.** Decide whether each matrix is invertible. Give a one-line reason (dependent columns, $Ax=0$, or $\det$).

(a) $\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{bmatrix}$

(b) $\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\2&4\end{bmatrix}$

(c) $\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}$

**C9.3.** True or false, with a one-line reason: for a single right-hand side $b$, forming $A^{-1}$ explicitly and then computing $A^{-1}b$ is generally preferable to solving $Ax=b$ with a solver / elimination.

**C9.4.** Let
$$
A=\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 2 & 0 \\
2 & 4 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}.
$$
The RREF of $A$ is
$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 2 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 1 \\
0 & 0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}
$$
(after row swaps / scaling as needed — use this RREF). Find $\operatorname{rank}(A)$, a basis for $C(A)$ using original columns, and a basis for $N(A)$.

**C9.5.** Let
$$
A=\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 \\
2 & 0 \\
0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}.
$$
Describe $C(A)$ in words. Is $b=(1,2,3)$ in $C(A)$? Find a basis for $N(A)$ (or state that $N(A)=\{0\}$).

**C9.6.** True or false, with a one-line reason: if $\operatorname{rank}(A)=n$ for an $m\times n$ matrix with $m\ge n$, then $Ax=0$ has only the zero solution.

**C9.7.** Compute each determinant.

(a) $\det\begin{bmatrix}3&1\\0&2\end{bmatrix}$

(b) $\det\begin{bmatrix}1&4&0\\0&-2&5\\0&0&3\end{bmatrix}$

(c) $\det\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\4&2\end{bmatrix}$

**C9.8.** Let $A=\begin{bmatrix}0&-2\\1&0\end{bmatrix}$. Compute $\det A$. Where do $e_1$ and $e_2$ go under $x\mapsto Ax$? What area does the unit square map to?

**C9.9.** True or false, with a one-line reason: if $\det A=0$ for square $A$, then $A$ is not invertible and $Ax=b$ fails to have a unique solution for every $b$.

**C9.10.** Connect the ideas. For square $A$, match each statement to the equivalent view (write the letter):

- (i) $A$ is invertible
- (ii) $\operatorname{rank}(A)=n$ (full)
- (iii) $\det A\neq 0$
- (iv) $N(A)=\{0\}$

Which of (ii)–(iv) are equivalent to (i)?

**C9.11 (cumulative, light).** Let $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{bmatrix}$. What geometric map is $x\mapsto Ax$? Is $A$ invertible? What is $\det A$?
