The question

You get (or build) three grayscale frames \(F_1,F_2,F_3\), each an \(m\times n\) matrix of intensities.

Compare two encoding strategies with rank-\(k\) SVD approximations:

Plan What you compress with a rank-\(k\) SVD
A — Independent frames \(F_1\), \(F_2\), and \(F_3\) separately
B — Frame + differences \(F_1\), then \(D_2=F_2-F_1\), then \(D_3=F_3-F_2\)

Driving question: For your data, when (if ever) is Plan B better than Plan A on accuracy, storage, and runtime / resource use — including the cost of computing the SVDs?

Computing an SVD is not free. Low-rank storage or faster later multiplies only help when the matrix is well approximated at modest \(k\) and you reuse the representation enough (or care enough about storage/transmission) to justify the factorization cost. Image examples are instructional.

What to turn in (Canvas)

One PDF (or short report + clearly labeled figures), individual submission, about 1–2 pages of writing plus figures/tables:

  1. Data. Where the three frames came from (phone stills, course sample, synthetic frames, etc.) and size \(m\times n\).
  2. Methods. Rank(s) \(k\) you tried; how you formed \(D_2,D_3\); how you rebuilt approximate frames under Plan B.
  3. Evidence (required numbers).
    • Relative reconstruction error vs \(k\) for Plan A and Plan B (plot or table).
    • Storage count: full \(mn\) vs rank-\(k\) cost \(\approx k(m+n+1)\) per compressed matrix (and totals for A vs B).
    • Timing: at least one honest runtime comparison (e.g. SVD time vs applying a low-rank map, or Plan A vs B factorization time for the same \(k\)).
  4. Conclusion. One clear recommendation: for your frames, prefer A, prefer B, or “depends,” with a quantitative reason and one limitation.

Gallery: 2–3 minute lightning talk or a station with one figure people can read in 30 seconds. Peers may ask about your numbers.

Technical notes

Storage (order of magnitude).

  • One full frame: \(mn\) numbers.
  • One rank-\(k\) factorization: about \(k(m+n+1)\) numbers (columns of \(U_k\), \(V_k\), and \(k\) singular values).
  • Plan A total (three frames): about \(3k(m+n+1)\).
  • Plan B total (three matrices): also about \(3k(m+n+1)\) at the same \(k\) — so Plan B wins on storage only if you can use a smaller \(k\) for differences while matching Plan A’s error, or if you choose different \(k\) per matrix on purpose.

Error. For a matrix \(A\) and approximation \(A_k\), a standard relative Frobenius error is \[ \frac{\lVert A-A_k\rVert_F}{\lVert A\rVert_F}. \] Under Plan B, measure error on the reconstructed frames \(\widehat F_2=\widehat F_1+\widehat D_2\), etc., not only on the difference matrices.

Software sketch

Task Octave Python
SVD [U,S,V] = svd(A) U, S, Vt = np.linalg.svd(A, full_matrices=False)
Rank-\(k\) rebuild first \(k\) columns / \(\sigma\)’s U[:,:k] @ np.diag(S[:k]) @ Vt[:k,:]
Frobenius norm norm(A,'fro') np.linalg.norm(A, 'fro')

Use grayscale (2D arrays). Color is optional enrichment only.
