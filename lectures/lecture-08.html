Lecture 08 — Angle and similarity

Mon Sep 14

September 13, 2026

Last updated: September 13, 2026.

Reading: Boyd 3.4 (angle). Quiz 3 prep: Boyd 3.23, 3.24 (angle vs distance). Related objectives: Lectures. Schedule. Quiz 3 is Friday, September 18.

Bring paper. Warm-up while attendance is taken, then fill-in notes, board examples, and Check yourself.

Learning objectives

By the end of class, students should be able to:

  1. Compute cosine similarity or an angle between vectors.
  2. Distinguish closeness in position from similarity in direction.
  3. Choose an appropriate similarity measure for a stated purpose.
  4. Explain why normalization may change a comparison.

Warm-up

Let \(a=(1,0)\) and \(b=(1,1)\).

  1. Compute \(a^{\mathsf{T}}b\), \(\lVert a\rVert\), and \(\lVert b\rVert\).

  2. Without finding the angle in degrees, is \(\cos\theta = a^{\mathsf{T}}b/(\lVert a\rVert\lVert b\rVert)\) closer to \(1\), to \(0\), or to \(-1\)? What does that suggest about the directions?

Fill in

For nonzero \(n\)-vectors \(a\) and \(b\), the angle \(\theta=\angle(a,b)\) satisfies \[ \cos\theta = \frac{a^{\mathsf{T}}b}{\lVert a\rVert\,\lVert b\rVert}, \qquad \theta\in[0,\pi]. \] The quantity \(\dfrac{a^{\mathsf{T}}b}{\lVert a\rVert\,\lVert b\rVert}\) is the cosine similarity.

Cauchy–Schwarz says \(|a^{\mathsf{T}}b|\leq\lVert a\rVert\lVert b\rVert\), so the cosine is always between \(\underline{\hspace{1.6cm}}\) and \(\underline{\hspace{1.6cm}}\).

Interpretation: - \(\theta\approx 0\) (cosine near \(1\)): directions nearly the \(\underline{\hspace{2.0cm}}\) - \(\theta=\pi/2\) (cosine \(0\)): vectors are \(\underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}\) - \(\theta\approx\pi\) (cosine near \(-1\)): directions nearly \(\underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}\)

Distance \(\lVert a-b\rVert\) measures closeness of positions. Angle measures similarity of directions (independent of overall scale of each vector). Scaling \(a\) by a positive constant does \(\underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}\) change \(\angle(a,b)\).

A normalized (unit) vector is \(\hat{a}=a/\lVert a\rVert\), which has \(\lVert\hat{a}\rVert=\underline{\hspace{1.0cm}}\). Among unit vectors, smaller distance is equivalent to \(\underline{\hspace{2.8cm}}\) angle (Boyd 3.24(b)).

Software

Octave:

a = [1; 0]; b = [1; 1];
cos_th = (a'*b)/(norm(a)*norm(b))
th_deg = acosd(cos_th)    % angle in degrees

Python:

import numpy as np
a = np.array([1.0, 0.0]); b = np.array([1.0, 1.0])
cos_th = (a @ b) / (np.linalg.norm(a) * np.linalg.norm(b))
th_deg = np.degrees(np.arccos(np.clip(cos_th, -1, 1)))

Worked in class

W1. Compute the cosine and the angle (degrees is fine) between \(u=(2,0)\) and \(v=(1,1)\). Sketch both from the origin.

W2. Distance vs direction. Let \(p=(1,1)\), \(q=(10,10)\), and \(r=(1,-1)\).

  1. Compute \(\lVert p-q\rVert\) and \(\lVert p-r\rVert\). Which is closer in distance to \(p\)?

  2. Compute \(\angle(p,q)\) and \(\angle(p,r)\). Which is more similar in direction to \(p\)?

  3. In one sentence: when would you prefer angle over Euclidean distance?

W3. Nonnegative vectors (Boyd 3.23). Let \(x=(2,1)\) and \(y=(0,3)\).

  1. Compute \(x^{\mathsf{T}}y\). Why must the angle be between \(0^\circ\) and \(90^\circ\)?

  2. When are two nonzero nonnegative vectors orthogonal?

W4. Distance nearest neighbor vs angle nearest neighbor (Boyd 3.24(a)). Let \[ x=(1,0),\qquad z_1=(2,0),\qquad z_2=(0.5,0.5). \] Find the distance nearest neighbor of \(x\) among \(\{z_1,z_2\}\) and the angle nearest neighbor. Are they the same?

W5. Normalization. Replace \(z_1\) and \(z_2\) by their unit versions \(\hat{z}_1=z_1/\lVert z_1\rVert\) and \(\hat{z}_2=z_2/\lVert z_2\rVert\). Does distance-NN of \(x\) among the unit vectors match angle-NN? (Boyd 3.24(b) idea.)

Check yourself

C1. Cosine of the angle between \((3,4)\) and \((4,-3)\). Are they orthogonal?

C2. True or false: if \(\lVert a-b\rVert\) is small, then \(\angle(a,b)\) must be near \(0\). One counterexample or reason.

C3. Document word-count vectors (nonnegative). Why is angle a common similarity for documents?

C4. Octave or Python: write one line that prints the cosine similarity of a and b (assume they already exist).

C5. You normalize every candidate before using Euclidean distance to a query. What comparison are you effectively making?

NoteAnswers (Check yourself only)

C1. \(a^{\mathsf{T}}b=12-12=0\), so cosine \(=0\). Yes, orthogonal (\(90^\circ\)).

C2. False. Example: \(a=(100,0)\), \(b=(101,0)\) are close in distance but that alone does not force a general claim for arbitrary nearby pairs that point opposite ways after a shift; clearer counterexample for “small distance \(\Rightarrow\) small angle”: take \(a=(0.01,0)\) and \(b=(-0.01,0)\) — distance \(0.02\) is small, but angle is \(180^\circ\).

C3. Documents can have very different lengths (total word counts). Angle / cosine ignores overall scale and focuses on the pattern of which words appear together.

C4. Octave: (a'*b)/(norm(a)*norm(b)). Python: (a @ b)/(np.linalg.norm(a)*np.linalg.norm(b)).

C5. Angle nearest-neighbor (among those candidates): after normalizing, Euclidean nearest matches smallest angle.

## Fill in

For nonzero $n$-vectors $a$ and $b$, the **angle** $\theta=\angle(a,b)$ satisfies
$$
\cos\theta = \frac{a^{\mathsf{T}}b}{\lVert a\rVert\,\lVert b\rVert}, \qquad \theta\in[0,\pi].
$$
The quantity $\dfrac{a^{\mathsf{T}}b}{\lVert a\rVert\,\lVert b\rVert}$ is the **cosine similarity**.

Cauchy–Schwarz says $|a^{\mathsf{T}}b|\leq\lVert a\rVert\lVert b\rVert$, so the cosine is always between $\underline{\hspace{1.6cm}}$ and $\underline{\hspace{1.6cm}}$.

Interpretation:
- $\theta\approx 0$ (cosine near $1$): directions nearly the $\underline{\hspace{2.0cm}}$
- $\theta=\pi/2$ (cosine $0$): vectors are $\underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}$
- $\theta\approx\pi$ (cosine near $-1$): directions nearly $\underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}$

**Distance** $\lVert a-b\rVert$ measures closeness of **positions**. **Angle** measures similarity of **directions** (independent of overall scale of each vector). Scaling $a$ by a positive constant does $\underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}$ change $\angle(a,b)$.

A **normalized** (unit) vector is $\hat{a}=a/\lVert a\rVert$, which has $\lVert\hat{a}\rVert=\underline{\hspace{1.0cm}}$. Among unit vectors, smaller distance is equivalent to $\underline{\hspace{2.8cm}}$ angle (Boyd 3.24(b)).
