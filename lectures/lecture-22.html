Learning objectives

By the end of class, you should be able to:

  1. Read a reduced row echelon form: name pivot and free variables and write a parametric solution.
  2. Tell inconsistent, unique, and infinitely many solutions apart from that form.
  3. Write an evolving system as \(x_{t+1}=Ax_t\) and build the transition matrix from a flow description.
  4. Compute and interpret the next state or two.

Checking a software solve with the residual \(Ax-b\) is mentioned below and practiced in Lab 2 and Lab 3. Many time steps, plots, and long-run patterns wait for the simulation lecture.

Warm-up

The RREF of an augmented matrix is \[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 & | & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & | & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & | & 0 \end{bmatrix}. \] Which variables are pivots? Which are free? Write the general solution.

Fill in

Reduced row echelon form (RREF) is echelon form with these extras:

  • Each pivot is \(\underline{\hspace{0.8cm}}\).
  • Each pivot is the \(\underline{\hspace{1.6cm}}\) nonzero in its column (zeros above and below).

A pivot variable corresponds to a pivot column. A free variable has \(\underline{\hspace{2.0cm}}\) pivot in its column; assign a parameter (for example \(x_3=t\)).

Reading the RREF of \([A\mid b]\):

Pattern Conclusion
A row \([0\ \cdots\ 0\mid c]\) with \(c\neq 0\) \(\underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}\)
Every column of \(A\) has a pivot, and the system is consistent \(\underline{\hspace{2.0cm}}\) solution
Consistent, and at least one free variable \(\underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}\) solutions

A row of zeros including the right-hand side is redundant, not inconsistent. Software can compute RREF or a solution (rref, A\b, numpy.linalg.solve). Verify a candidate \(x\) by the residual \(Ax-b\), which should be near \(\underline{\hspace{1.0cm}}\).

A linear dynamical system (discrete time) has the form \[ x_{t+1}=\underline{\hspace{1.6cm}}, \] where \(t=0,1,2,\ldots\) is the time index.

  • The state \(x_t\) is an \(n\)-vector that describes the system at time \(t\).
  • The matrix \(A\) is the transition matrix. Its size is \(\underline{\hspace{1.6cm}}\).

Columns. Column \(j\) of \(A\) is the next state if the current state is \(\underline{\hspace{2.0cm}}\).

A few steps by hand. \(x_1=Ax_0\), \(x_2=Ax_1\), and so on. Packaging \(t\) steps as \(A^t\) comes later. To build \(A\) from a flow, ask: if everything starts in compartment \(j\), where does it go next? Those destinations are column \(j\).

Worked in class

W1. Classify from RREF. Unique, none, or infinitely many?

  1. \[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & | & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & | & -1 \end{bmatrix} \]

  2. \[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & | & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & | & 1 \end{bmatrix} \]

  3. \[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 & | & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & | & 4 \end{bmatrix} \]

W2. Parametric form. From \[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 & 0 & | & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & | & -1 \end{bmatrix}, \] name the free variable and write \(x=(x_1,x_2,x_3)\) in terms of a parameter.

W3. Build a transition matrix. Two towns, Arcata and Eureka, exchange residents each year:

  • Of those in Arcata, \(80\%\) stay and \(20\%\) move to Eureka.
  • Of those in Eureka, \(90\%\) stay and \(10\%\) move to Arcata.

Order the state as \(x_t=(\text{Arcata},\text{Eureka})\). Write \(A\). With \(x_0=(5000,7000)\), compute \(x_1\). Does Arcata rise or fall in that year?

W4 (if time). State and matrix. A campus tracks (on-campus, off-campus) as \(x_t\) with \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}0.7&0.15\\ 0.3&0.85\end{bmatrix}. \] (a) What does \(A_{12}\) mean in words? (b) What does the first column represent? (c) If \(x_0=(800,200)\), compute \(x_1\) and interpret both entries as people.

W5 (if time). One residual. For \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{bmatrix},\quad b=\begin{bmatrix}5\\11\end{bmatrix}, \] solve \(Ax=b\) with Octave or Python and compute \(Ax-b\).

Your turn

Start with Y1–Y3 and Y5–Y6. The others are extra practice of the same skills.

Y1. From the RREF \[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & -2 & | & 5 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & | & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & | & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \] list pivot variables, free variables, and the general solution.

Y2. True or false: if RREF has a row of all zeros including the augmented entry, the system is inconsistent.

Y3. A \(3\times 5\) matrix \(A\), after a consistent RREF of \([A\mid b]\), has \(3\) pivots. How many free variables? Unique solution or infinitely many?

Y4. You solve \(Ax=b\) in software and get \(x=(1,-1)\). You compute \(Ax-b=(0.0,\,10^{-15})\). Was the solve successful? Why check the residual?

Y5. True or false, with a one-line reason: if \(x_{t+1}=Ax_t\), then knowing \(x_t\) and \(A\) determines \(x_{t+1}\) uniquely.

Y6. Two warehouses, \(x_t=(\text{A},\text{B})\). Each day, A keeps \(70\%\) and ships \(30\%\) to B; B keeps \(60\%\) and ships \(40\%\) to A. Write \(A\). With \(x_0=(100,50)\), find \(x_1\).

Y7. With \(A=\begin{bmatrix}1.1&0\\0&0.5\end{bmatrix}\) and \(x_0=(2,8)\), compute \(x_1\) and \(x_2\). Which component is growing? Which is shrinking?

Y8 (if time). Three age groups (youth, adult, senior): \[ A=\begin{bmatrix} 0.6 & 0 & 0 \\ 0.4 & 0.7 & 0 \\ 0 & 0.3 & 0.8 \end{bmatrix},\qquad x_0=(100,200,50). \] Interpret \(A_{21}\) and \(A_{33}\) in one phrase each, and compute \(x_1\).

NoteAnswers (Your turn only)

Y1. Pivot variables \(x_1,x_2\); free \(x_3=t\). Then \(x_1-2t=5\) and \(x_2+3t=-1\), so \[ x=(5+2t,\,-1-3t,\,t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}. \]

Y2. False. A zero row including the right-hand side is consistent (a redundant equation). Inconsistency needs \([0\ \cdots\ 0\mid c]\) with \(c\neq 0\).

Y3. \(n=5\) unknowns and \(3\) pivots \(\Rightarrow\) \(2\) free variables \(\Rightarrow\) infinitely many solutions (when consistent).

Y4. Yes — the residual is numerically zero (roundoff). Checking \(Ax-b\) catches a wrong \(x\), a singular system, or a copy-paste mistake. Fuller software practice is Lab 3.

Y5. True. The rule \(x_{t+1}=Ax_t\) is a function of the current state: one matrix–vector product gives the unique next state.

Y6. \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}0.7&0.4\\ 0.3&0.6\end{bmatrix},\qquad x_1=\begin{bmatrix}90\\60\end{bmatrix}. \] Column 1: keep \(70\%\) in A and ship \(30\%\) to B. Column 2: ship \(40\%\) of B to A and keep \(60\%\) in B.

Y7. \(x_1=(2.2,4)\), \(x_2=(2.42,2)\). The first component grows (factor \(1.1\) each step); the second shrinks (factor \(0.5\)).

Y8. \(A_{21}=0.4\): the factor sending youth to adults in one step. \(A_{33}=0.8\): the factor of seniors who remain seniors. \[ x_1=Ax_0=\begin{bmatrix}60\\180\\100\end{bmatrix}. \]
