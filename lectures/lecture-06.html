Lecture 06 — Linear functions in applications

Fri Sep 4

Modified

September 2, 2026

Download PDF

Last updated: September 2, 2026.

Reading: Boyd 2.1–2.3 applications (score, cost, temperature, regression). Quiz 2 prep: Boyd 1.12, 1.14, 1.15. Related objectives: Lectures. Schedule. Quiz 2 is Friday, September 11.

Bring paper. Warm-up while attendance is taken, then fill-in notes, board examples, and Check yourself.

Learning objectives

By the end of class, students should be able to:

  1. Model a score, cost, prediction, or aggregate quantity.
  2. Interpret model coefficients and intercepts.
  3. Compare outputs from competing models.
  4. State a reasonable limitation or assumption of a linear model.

Warm-up

Is \(f(x)=5x_1+2x_2\) linear, affine, or both? What about \(g(x)=5x_1+2x_2-3\)?

Fill in

An affine model \(f(x)=a^{\mathsf{T}}x+b\) often approximates a real quantity only over a limited range of inputs; outside that range the model may be \(\underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}\).

In a cost model \(c^{\mathsf{T}}q\), the entry \(c_i\) is the cost per unit of material \(i\). The total cost is a \(\underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}\) of the amounts \(q_i\).

In Boyd 1.14, \(f^{\mathsf{T}}h\) is sector exposure: net dollars invested in assets with \(f_i=1\). If \(f^{\mathsf{T}}h=0\), the portfolio is \(\underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}\) to that sector.

To pick the cheapest of \(K\) suppliers with price vectors \(p_1,\ldots,p_K\) for order \(q\), compare \(p_1^{\mathsf{T}}q,\ldots,p_K^{\mathsf{T}}q\) and choose the \(\underline{\hspace{2.0cm}}\).

A regression model \(\hat{y}=\beta^{\mathsf{T}}x+v\) is useful when the relationship is roughly \(\underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}\) over the data range, but coefficients can be hard to interpret if features are \(\underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}\).

Worked in class

W1. A 30-question magazine quiz uses answers \(a_i\in\{1,2,3\}\) (Rarely, Sometimes, Often). Questions 1–15: Sometimes \(+1\), Often \(+2\). Questions 16–30: Sometimes \(+2\), Often \(+4\). Rarely adds 0. Write total score \(s=w^{\mathsf{T}}a+v\) (Boyd 2.6).

W2. Cash held in five currencies is \(c=(1000,500,-200,0,300)\) (USD, RMB, EUR, GBP, JPY). Exchange rates to USD are \(r=(1,0.14,1.08,1.27,0.0067)\). Total USD value is \(r^{\mathsf{T}}c\) (Boyd 1.12). Compute it (negative EUR is a liability).

W3. Three assets; pharma indicator \(f=(1,0,1)\). Portfolio \(h=(500,200,-100)\) dollars (short on asset 2). Compute exposure \(f^{\mathsf{T}}h\) (Boyd 1.14). In words, what does the sign mean?

W4. Order \(q=(10,5,20)\) of three materials. Supplier A prices \(p_A=(2,4,1)\); supplier B prices \(p_B=(3,2,2)\) per unit (Boyd 1.15). Which supplier is cheaper for the whole order? Compare \(p_A^{\mathsf{T}}q\) and \(p_B^{\mathsf{T}}q\).

W5. Processor temperature is affine in power: \(T=a^{\mathsf{T}}P+b\). Idle \(P=(10,10,10)\) gives \(T=30\). Full power on processor 1 only, \(P=(100,10,10)\), gives \(T=60\). Find \(a_1\) from those two readings (Boyd 2.2 setup).

Check yourself

C1. Two regression models for the same house: \(\hat{y}_A=200{,}000+120x_1+15{,}000x_2\) and \(\hat{y}_B=180{,}000+130x_1+18{,}000x_2\), where \(x_1\) is area (1000 ft²) and \(x_2\) is bedrooms. For \(x=(2,3)\), compute both predictions. Which predicts higher?

C2. Boyd 2.4 style: \(\varphi(1,1,0)=-1\), \(\varphi(-1,1,1)=1\), \(\varphi(1,-1,-1)=1\). Could \(\varphi\) be linear? Could it be affine? Brief reason.

C3. You must buy \(q=(4,2)\). Supplier 1: \(p_1=(5,3)\); supplier 2: \(p_2=(4,5)\). Which supplier wins on total cost \(p_k^{\mathsf{T}}q\)?

C4. A long-only portfolio (\(h_i\geq 0\)) is neutral to pharma (\(f^{\mathsf{T}}h=0\) with \(f_i\in\{0,1\}\)). In one sentence, what does that mean about pharma holdings?

C5. Name one reasonable limitation of using a linear model for café revenue \(p^{\mathsf{T}}x\) when \(x\) counts drinks sold.

NoteAnswers (Check yourself only)

C1. \(\hat{y}_A=200{,}000+120\cdot 2+15{,}000\cdot 3=245{,}240\). \(\hat{y}_B=180{,}000+130\cdot 2+18{,}000\cdot 3=248{,}260\). Model B predicts higher.

C2. Cannot be linear: if \(\varphi(x)=a^{\mathsf{T}}x\), the three given values force an inconsistent system for \(a\). Could be affine: many choices of \(a,b\) with \(\varphi(x)=a^{\mathsf{T}}x+b\) fit three input–output pairs.

C3. \(p_1^{\mathsf{T}}q=5\cdot 4+3\cdot 2=26\). \(p_2^{\mathsf{T}}q=4\cdot 4+5\cdot 2=26\). Tie.

C4. No net dollar holdings in pharma stocks (long positions in pharma are fully offset by non-pharma or the portfolio holds none).

C5. Sample: prices fixed but demand may saturate; loss leaders; does not model quantity discounts; assumes all drinks sold at listed price.
