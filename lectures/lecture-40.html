Learning objectives

By the end of class, students should be able to:

  1. Interpret \(A=U\Sigma V^{\mathsf{T}}\) conceptually.
  2. Construct a rank-\(k\) approximation computationally.
  3. Compare singular-value decay for an image/frame and a frame-difference matrix.
  4. Encode three frames either independently or as \(F_1\), \(D_2\), \(D_3\).
  5. Compare reconstruction error and storage for the two approaches.
  6. Formulate a capstone question involving accuracy, storage, runtime, and resource use.

(Objectives 3–6 are launched today and carried in the capstone packet; Lab 6 is least squares, not SVD coding.)

Warm-up (after quiz)

A grayscale image (or one video frame) is an \(m\times n\) matrix of pixel intensities.

  1. Roughly how many numbers store the full matrix?

  2. If most “information” lived in a few patterns, why might a factored low-rank form save storage?

Fill in

SVD (conceptual). Every real \(m\times n\) matrix factors as \[ A = U\Sigma V^{\mathsf{T}}, \] where the columns of \(U\) and \(V\) are orthonormal (left / right singular vectors) and \(\Sigma\) is rectangular “diagonal” with nonnegative singular values \[ \sigma_1\ge\sigma_2\ge\cdots\ge 0 \] on its main diagonal. Think: \(A\) = rotate/reflect → stretch by \(\sigma_i\) → rotate/reflect.

Rank-\(k\) approximation. Keep the largest \(k\) singular values (and matching singular-vector columns); set the rest to zero. The truncated product \[ A_k = U_k\Sigma_k V_k^{\mathsf{T}} \] is the best rank-\(k\) approximation in the usual 2-norm / Frobenius sense (details in the slides / capstone).

Software sketch (capstone / later — not Lab 6 today):

Task Octave Python
SVD [U,S,V] = svd(A) U, S, Vt = np.linalg.svd(A, full_matrices=False)
Rank-\(k\) rebuild use first \(k\) columns / \(\sigma\)’s U[:,:k] @ np.diag(S[:k]) @ Vt[:k,:]

Capstone launch (one question). For a short clip of frames: when is it better to compress each frame alone versus encode \(F_1\) and differences \(D_2=F_2-F_1\), \(D_3=F_3-F_2\)? Trade off accuracy, storage, runtime, and resource use — including the cost of computing the SVD itself. Full prompts live in the capstone packet; studio is Mon Dec 7.

Worked in class (short)

W1. Read \(\Sigma\). Suppose a \(4\times 3\) matrix has singular values \(\sigma=(10,\,2,\,0.1)\). What is \(\operatorname{rank}(A)\) (numerically “full” vs tiny)? For a rank-\(1\) approximation, which \(\sigma\) do you keep?

W2. Storage cartoon. Full \(m\times n\) needs \(mn\) entries. A rank-\(k\) factorization stores about \(k(m+n+1)\) numbers (columns of \(U_k\), \(V_k\), and \(k\) singular values). When is \(k(m+n+1)\ll mn\)?

W3. Frames vs differences (launch). Three frames \(F_1,F_2,F_3\). Plan A: compress each. Plan B: compress \(F_1\), \(D_2=F_2-F_1\), \(D_3=F_3-F_2\). In one sentence each: when might Plan B win on storage? What must you still check (error / runtime)?

NoteAnswers (Your turn only)

Y1. Stretch factors / importance of each singular-vector mode (how strongly \(A\) amplifies that direction).

Y2. False. The factorization itself can be expensive; low-rank reuse only pays off when approximation quality is good and you reuse the factors enough (see capstone notes).

Y3. Quiz 10 only.

Y4. Any clear tradeoff question is fine — e.g. “For my three frames, does difference encoding cut storage at the same relative error, after counting SVD time?”
