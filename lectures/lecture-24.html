Lecture 24 — Linear dynamical systems

Mon Oct 19

Modified

October 8, 2026

Download PDF

Last updated: October 8, 2026.

Reading: Boyd 9.1–9.3. Related objectives: Lectures. Schedule.

Bring paper. Warm-up while attendance is taken, then fill-in notes, board examples, and Your turn.

Learning objectives

By the end of class, students should be able to:

  1. Represent an evolving system as \(x_{t+1}=Ax_t\).
  2. Interpret the state vector and transition matrix.
  3. Build a transition matrix from a flow description.
  4. Compute and interpret the next few states.

Warm-up

From Lecture 19, the matrix \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}0.9&0.2\\ 0.1&0.8\end{bmatrix} \] sends (urban, rural) population fractions today to tomorrow. If today’s state is \(x_0=(0.6,0.4)\), compute \(x_1=Ax_0\). In one sentence: what do the two entries of \(x_1\) mean?

Fill in

A linear dynamical system (discrete time) has the form \[ x_{t+1}=\underline{\hspace{1.6cm}}, \] where \(t=0,1,2,\ldots\) is the time index.

  • The state \(x_t\) is an \(n\)-vector that completely describes the system at time \(t\).
  • The matrix \(A\) is the dynamics / transition matrix. Its size is \(\underline{\hspace{1.6cm}}\) (same \(n\) as the state).

Interpretation of columns. The \(j\)th column of \(A\) is the next state if the current state is \(\underline{\hspace{2.0cm}}\) (one unit in compartment \(j\), nothing elsewhere).

A few steps by hand. \[ x_1=Ax_0,\qquad x_2=Ax_1=A(Ax_0),\qquad x_3=Ax_2=\cdots. \] (We will package “\(t\) steps at once” as \(A^t\) later; today focus on one step at a time.)

Building \(A\) from a flow story. For each compartment \(j\), ask: if everything starts in \(j\), where does it go next? Those destinations become column \(j\).

Worked in class

W1. State and matrix. A campus tracks (on-campus residents, off-campus) each week as a \(2\)-vector \(x_t\). Someone proposes \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}0.7&0.15\\ 0.3&0.85\end{bmatrix}. \] (a) What does entry \(A_{12}\) mean in words? (b) What does the first column of \(A\) represent? (c) If \(x_0=(800,200)\), compute \(x_1\) and interpret both entries (people, not fractions).

W2. Build \(A\) from flows. Two towns, Arcata and Eureka, exchange residents each year:

  • Of those in Arcata, \(80\%\) stay and \(20\%\) move to Eureka.
  • Of those in Eureka, \(90\%\) stay and \(10\%\) move to Arcata.

Order the state as \(x_t=(\text{Arcata},\text{Eureka})\). Write the \(2\times 2\) transition matrix \(A\).

W3. Next few states. With \(A\) from W2 and \(x_0=(5000,7000)\), compute \(x_1\) and \(x_2\) by hand. Does the Arcata population rise or fall over these two years?

W4. Epidemic sketch (Boyd-style). Let \(x_t=(S_t,I_t)\) be (susceptible, infected) counts among a fixed population of size \(N=S_t+I_t\). A simple model uses \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}0.95&0.10\\ 0.05&0.90\end{bmatrix}. \] (a) Check that each column sums to \(1\). Why is that natural here? (b) If \(x_0=(190,10)\), compute \(x_1\). Did infections go up or down?

Your turn

Y1. True or false, with a one-line reason: if \(x_{t+1}=Ax_t\), then knowing \(x_t\) and \(A\) determines \(x_{t+1}\) uniquely.

Y2. Three age groups (youth, adult, senior) with transition \[ A=\begin{bmatrix} 0.6 & 0 & 0 \\ 0.4 & 0.7 & 0 \\ 0 & 0.3 & 0.8 \end{bmatrix}. \] Interpret \(A_{21}\) and \(A_{33}\) in one phrase each. If \(x_0=(100,200,50)\), compute \(x_1\).

Y3. Build \(A\). Two warehouse inventory levels \(x_t=(\text{A},\text{B})\). Each day:

  • Warehouse A keeps \(70\%\) of its stock and ships \(30\%\) to B.
  • Warehouse B keeps \(60\%\) and ships \(40\%\) to A.

Write \(A\). Then with \(x_0=(100,50)\), find \(x_1\).

Y4. With \(A=\begin{bmatrix}1.1&0\\0&0.5\end{bmatrix}\) and \(x_0=(2,8)\), compute \(x_1\) and \(x_2\). Which component is growing? Which is shrinking?

NoteAnswers (Your turn only)

Y1. True. The rule \(x_{t+1}=Ax_t\) is a function of the current state: one matrix–vector product gives the unique next state.

Y2. \(A_{21}=0.4\): fraction (or count factor) of youth that become adults in one step. \(A_{33}=0.8\): fraction of seniors that remain seniors. \[ x_1=Ax_0=\begin{bmatrix}60\\180\\100\end{bmatrix}. \]

Y3. \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}0.7&0.4\\ 0.3&0.6\end{bmatrix},\qquad x_1=\begin{bmatrix}90\\60\end{bmatrix}. \] (Column 1: keep \(70\%\) in A, ship \(30\%\) to B. Column 2: ship \(40\%\) of B to A, keep \(60\%\) in B.)

Y4. \(x_1=(2.2,4)\), \(x_2=(2.42,2)\). First component grows (factor \(1.1\) each step); second shrinks (factor \(0.5\)).
