Lecture 02 — Vector arithmetic and geometry

Wed Aug 26

Modified

August 18, 2026

Download PDF

Last updated: August 18, 2026.

Reading: Boyd 1.2–1.3. Related objectives: Lectures. Schedule.

Learning objectives

By the end of class, students should be able to:

  1. Add, subtract, and scale vectors.
  2. Interpret those operations geometrically.
  3. Plot vectors in \(\mathbb{R}^2\) and recognize corresponding operations in \(\mathbb{R}^3\).
  4. Check whether an answer has sensible dimensions and units.

Fill in

If \(a\) and \(b\) are \(n\)-vectors, the sum \(a+b\) is the \(n\)-vector whose \(i\)th entry is \(\underline{\hspace{2.8cm}}\). Addition is only defined when \(\underline{\hspace{3.0cm}}\).

The identity for addition is \(\underline{\hspace{1.6cm}}\): \(a+0=a\). The difference \(a-b\) is \(\underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}\).

If \(\beta\) is a scalar, the scalar–vector product \(\beta a\) is the \(n\)-vector whose \(i\)th entry is \(\underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}\). In particular, \((-1)a\) is written \(\underline{\hspace{1.2cm}}\).

Geometry in \(\mathbb{R}^2\): if \(a\) is drawn as an arrow, \(\beta a\) with \(\beta>0\) points \(\underline{\hspace{2.0cm}}\) and has length scaled by \(\underline{\hspace{1.4cm}}\). If \(\beta<0\), the arrow points \(\underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}\).

Adding a scalar to a vector is \(\underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}\) in the mathematics of this course (even if some software will do it). Concatenating \((1,2)\) with \((3,4)\) is also \(\underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}\) vector addition.

Worked in class

W1. A point on the quad is \(p=(2,1)\) (east, north, in 10 m units). A walk is the displacement \(a=(3,-1)\). Compute \(p+a\) and sketch \(p\), \(a\), and \(p+a\) in \(\mathbb{R}^2\). Then compute \(a-p\). Does \(a-p\) have the same units as \(p\)?

W2. Two rain gauges, in inches, over three days: \[ r = (0.40, 0, 1.10), \qquad s = (0.15, 0.20, 0.30). \] Compute \(r+s\) (combined rainfall if you could pour both gauges into one story). Compute \(2r\). Why is \(r+(1,1)\) not a valid vector sum here?

W3. Sanity check: can you form \((4,1)+(0,2,5)\)? Can you form \((18\text{ cm}, 4\text{ mm})+(1,1)\) as a meaningful measurement vector?

Your turn

Y1. Compute \[ \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ -1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix} \qquad\text{and}\qquad (-2) \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0.5 \end{bmatrix}. \]

Y2. In \(\mathbb{R}^2\), sketch \(u=(3,1)\) and \(\tfrac{1}{2}u\). In words, what did scaling by \(1/2\) do? What would \(-u\) look like?

Y3. A 2-vector of temperatures in °C is \(x=(10, 14)\). A classmate types \(x+5\) in software and gets \((15,19)\). Is that the same as a vector sum \(x+v\) for some 2-vector \(v\)? If you need “five degrees warmer each day,” what vector should you add?

NoteAnswers (Your turn only)

Y1. \((5,2,0)\) and \((-2,-1)\).

Y2. \(\tfrac{1}{2}u\) is the same direction, half as long. \(-u\) is the opposite direction, same length.

Y3. Software added the scalar \(5\) to each entry; that is not a vector–vector sum. Adding five degrees each day is the vector sum \(x+(5,5)\).

## Your turn

**Y1.** Compute
$$
\begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ -1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}
+
\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(-2)
\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0.5 \end{bmatrix}.
