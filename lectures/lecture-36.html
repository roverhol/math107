Lecture 36 — Characteristic equation, \(2 imes 2\) eigenproblems, and complex numbers

Wed Nov 18

Modified

October 8, 2026

Download PDF

Last updated: October 8, 2026.

Reading: Strang §6.1, characteristic equation and the complex-eigenvalue (\(90^\circ\) rotation) example. Related objectives: Lectures. Schedule.

Bring paper. Warm-up while attendance is taken, then fill-in notes, board examples, and Your turn.

Learning objectives

By the end of class, students should be able to:

  1. Interpret \(a+bi\) algebraically and as a point/vector in the complex plane.
  2. Perform the basic complex arithmetic needed for eigenvalue calculations.
  3. Form and solve \(\det(A-\lambda I)=0\) for a \(2\times 2\) matrix.
  4. Find corresponding eigenvectors.
  5. Check real or complex eigenpairs by substitution.

Warm-up

Monday’s rotation \(R=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}\) had no real eigenvector.

  1. Form \(R-\lambda I\) and compute \(\det(R-\lambda I)\) as a quadratic in \(\lambda\).

  2. What are the roots? Are they real?

Fill in

Complex numbers. A complex number is \(z=a+bi\) with \(a,b\in\mathbb{R}\) and \(i^2=\underline{\hspace{0.8cm}}\). Plot \(z\) as the point \((a,b)\) in the complex plane.

Arithmetic (what we need):

  • Add/subtract: combine real and imaginary parts.
  • Multiply: \((a+bi)(c+di)=(ac-bd)+(ad+bc)i\).
  • Conjugate: \(\overline{a+bi}=\underline{\hspace{1.6cm}}\).
  • Magnitude: \(|a+bi|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\).

Characteristic equation. Eigenvalues of square \(A\) solve \[ \det(A-\lambda I)=0. \] For \(2\times 2\), this is a \(\underline{\hspace{1.6cm}}\) equation in \(\lambda\).

Eigenvectors. For each eigenvalue \(\lambda\), solve \[ (A-\lambda I)v=0,\qquad v\neq 0 \] (null space of \(A-\lambda I\)). Scale freely — any nonzero multiple is fine.

Check. Always verify \(Av=\lambda v\) by substitution (works for real or complex pairs).

Worked in class

W1. Complex drill. Let \(z=2+i\) and \(w=1-3i\).

  1. Compute \(z+w\), \(zw\), and \(\overline{z}\).
  2. Plot \(z\) and \(\overline{z}\) in the complex plane.

W2. Real \(2\times 2\) by hand. Let \[ A=\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix}. \]

  1. Expand \(\det(A-\lambda I)\) and solve for \(\lambda\).
  2. For each eigenvalue, find one eigenvector.
  3. Check one pair by computing \(Av\) and \(\lambda v\).

W3. Rotation eigenvalues. For \(R=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}\):

  1. Confirm the characteristic polynomial is \(\lambda^2+1=0\), so \(\lambda=\pm i\).
  2. For \(\lambda=i\), solve \((R-iI)v=0\) and give one complex eigenvector.
  3. Check \(Rv=iv\) by matrix–vector multiplication (complex arithmetic).

W4. Interpretation preview. Complex conjugate eigenvalues of a real matrix often signal \(\underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}\) (rotation / oscillation) in the real plane — Friday’s lab makes this concrete with software.

Your turn

Y1. Compute \((3-2i)(1+i)\) and \(|3-2i|\).

Y2. For \(A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\0&3\end{bmatrix}\), form \(\det(A-\lambda I)\), find both eigenvalues, and an eigenvector for \(\lambda=3\).

Y3. Check: with \(A=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}\) and \(v=(1,-i)\), verify \(Av=iv\) (or explain the correct \(\lambda\) if this \(v\) is for \(\lambda=-i\)).

Y4. True or false: every real \(2\times 2\) matrix has two real eigenvalues.

NoteAnswers (Your turn only)

Y1. \((3-2i)(1+i)=3+3i-2i-2i^2=5+i\). Magnitude: \(|3-2i|=\sqrt{9+4}=\sqrt{13}\).

Y2. \(A-\lambda I=\begin{bmatrix}1-\lambda&2\\0&3-\lambda\end{bmatrix}\), so \(\det=(1-\lambda)(3-\lambda)\). Eigenvalues \(\lambda=1\) and \(\lambda=3\). For \(\lambda=3\): \((A-3I)v=\begin{bmatrix}-2&2\\0&0\end{bmatrix}v=0\) gives \(v_1=v_2\), e.g. \(v=(1,1)\).

Y3. \(Av=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1\\-i\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}i\\1\end{bmatrix}\) and \(iv=\begin{bmatrix}i\\1\end{bmatrix}\). Yes — \(Av=iv\) (so this \(v\) matches \(\lambda=i\)).

Y4. False. The characteristic polynomial may have complex roots (e.g. \(90^\circ\) rotation: \(\lambda=\pm i\)).
