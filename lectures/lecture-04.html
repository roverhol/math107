Lecture 04 — Inner product

Mon Aug 31

Modified

August 31, 2026

Download PDF

Last updated: August 31, 2026.

Reading: Boyd 1.4 (inner product); Boyd 1.1 stack and slice (Quiz 1 prep).

Learning objectives

By the end of class, students should be able to:

  1. Compute a dot product.
  2. Interpret it as a weighted sum.
  3. Interpret its sign and magnitude as alignment.
  4. Recognize orthogonal vectors from a zero dot product.

Fill in — stack and slice

A stacked (or block) vector lists the entries of one vector after another. If \(b\) is an \(m\)-vector and \(c\) is an \(n\)-vector, then \[ (b, c) = (b_1,\ldots,b_m,\ c_1,\ldots,c_n) \] is an \((m+n)\)-vector. A scalar can be stacked with a vector: if \(a\) is a 3-vector, then \((1,a)\) is a \(\underline{\hspace{1.6cm}}\)-vector with first entry \(1\), then \(a_1,a_2,a_3\).

Stacking is not vector addition: \((a,b)\) has length \(m+n\), not \(m\).

A slice (or subvector) takes a contiguous block of entries. Boyd’s notation \(x_{r:s}\) means the vector of entries \(x_r, x_{r+1}, \ldots, x_s\). It has length \(\underline{\hspace{2.0cm}}\). The endpoints are inclusive.

Example: if \(z=(1,-1,2,0)\), then \(z_{2:3} = \underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}\).

If \(a\) and \(b\) are 10-vectors and \(c\) is a 20-vector, then \((a,b,c_{3:13})\) has length \(\underline{\hspace{2.0cm}}\).

Fill in

For \(n\)-vectors \(a\) and \(b\), the inner product (or dot product) is the scalar \[ a^{\mathsf{T}}b = a_1 b_1 + a_2 b_2 + \cdots + a_n b_n = \sum_{i=1}^n \underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}. \] It is only defined when \(a\) and \(b\) have the same \(\underline{\hspace{1.6cm}}\).

If \(w\) is a weight vector and \(x\) is a data vector (same length), then \(w^{\mathsf{T}}x\) is a \(\underline{\hspace{3.2cm}}\) of the entries of \(x\), using the weights in \(w\).

Geometrically in \(\mathbb{R}^2\) (and similarly in \(\mathbb{R}^3\)): if \(a^{\mathsf{T}}b>0\), the arrows point roughly the \(\underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}\) direction; if \(a^{\mathsf{T}}b<0\), they point roughly \(\underline{\hspace{2.0cm}}\) directions; if \(a^{\mathsf{T}}b=0\), the vectors are \(\underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}\).

Two vectors with \(a^{\mathsf{T}}b=0\) are orthogonal (perpendicular). The inner product is commutative: \(a^{\mathsf{T}}b = \underline{\hspace{2.0cm}}\).

The quantity \(a^{\mathsf{T}}a = a_1^2 + \cdots + a_n^2\) is always \(\underline{\hspace{1.6cm}}\) (never negative).

Worked in class

W1. Let \(b=(2,-1)\) and \(c=(0,3,1)\). Write the stacked vector \((b,c)\) explicitly. What is its length?

W2. Let \(z=(1,-1,2,0)\). Write \(z_{2:3}\) and \(z_{1:2}\). If \(x=(63,59,61,66)\) is four daily high temperatures (°F), write the 3-vector of day-to-day changes as \(x_{2:4} - x_{1:3}\).

W3. Compute \(u^{\mathsf{T}}v\) for \(u=(1,1)\) and \(v=(1,-1)\). Sketch both arrows in \(\mathbb{R}^2\) from the origin. How does the zero dot product match the picture?

W4. Three quiz parts have scores (out of 1): \[ g = (0.80,\ 0.90,\ 0.70), \] with weights \[ w = (0.50,\ 0.30,\ 0.20). \] Compute the weighted total \(w^{\mathsf{T}}g\). (Same idea as a weighted average when the weights sum to 1.)

W5. Morning wind displacements (east, north), in km/h: \[ a = (3, 1), \qquad b = (-1, 3). \] Compute \(a^{\mathsf{T}}b\). Is it positive, negative, or zero? In one sentence, what does the sign tell you about how the two directions line up?

Your turn

Y1. Let \(a=(4,1,2)\) and \(b=(1,0,1)\) be shopping lists (apples, bread, milk). Write \((a,b)\) and state its length.

Y2. Let \(c=(10,12,11,14,13)\) be five daily counts. Find \(c_{2:4}\).

Y3. Compute \((2,-1)^{\mathsf{T}}(3,4)\) and \((1,0)^{\mathsf{T}}(0,1)\).

Y4. A café tracks daily sales in the order (espresso, milk, syrup): \[ x = (40,\ 30,\ 10), \] with prices in dollars \[ p = (3,\ 2,\ 1). \] What quantity does \(p^{\mathsf{T}}x\) represent? Compute it.

Y5. Two shopping lists, in the order (apples, bread, milk): \[ r = (2,\ 1,\ 3), \qquad s = (4,\ 2,\ 6). \] Compute \(r^{\mathsf{T}}s\). Notice anything about \(r\) and \(s\)? If \(t=(1,0,-1)\), compute \(r^{\mathsf{T}}t\) and say whether \(r\) and \(t\) are orthogonal.

NoteAnswers (Your turn only)

Y1. \((a,b)=(4,1,2,1,0,1)\); length \(6\).

Y2. \(c_{2:4}=(12,11,14)\).

Y3. \((2,-1)^{\mathsf{T}}(3,4)=2\cdot 3+(-1)\cdot 4=2\). \((1,0)^{\mathsf{T}}(0,1)=0\) (orthogonal).

Y4. Total revenue in dollars: \(p^{\mathsf{T}}x=3\cdot 40+2\cdot 30+1\cdot 10=120+60+10=190\) dollars.

Y5. \(r^{\mathsf{T}}s=2\cdot 4+1\cdot 2+3\cdot 6=8+2+18=28\). Also \(s=2r\), so the lists are proportional (same “direction” in data space). \(r^{\mathsf{T}}t=2\cdot 1+1\cdot 0+3\cdot(-1)=2-3=-1\neq 0\), so \(r\) and \(t\) are not orthogonal.
